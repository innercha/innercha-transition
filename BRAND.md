# Innercha Brand Worksheet

This is the working identity document for Phase 2. It should guide visuals and language without over-defining the music.

## Core meaning

**Innercha** draws from:

- inertia
- inner motion
- inner momentum
- the phonetic influence of Kercha
- movement through internal, emotional, and psychedelic spaces

Public explanations do not need to unpack every syllable. A concise meaning such as **“inspired by the idea of inner motion”** preserves clarity and mystery.

The exact meaning of "cha" is intentionally left undefined. Pinning it down would drift toward etymology rather than the ambiguity that gives the name room to grow — the same way names like Bonobo or Tycho aren't literal.

### Finalized core themes (2026-07-29)

Narrowed from a longer personal list through direct reflection, not invented from scratch:

1. **Inner motion** — everything is changing; even stillness contains motion.
2. **Transformation** — music, people, nature, and identity are not static.
3. **Perception** — curiosity, wonder, seeing familiar things differently.
4. **Organic systems** — nature, ecology, feedback loops, growth, emergence.
5. **Emotional depth** — lived-in, honest, melancholic, hopeful, human.

**Flow state** and **inner paths to outer space** are supporting phrases and signature language, not pillars — flow state is a consequence of the themes above, not a philosophy of its own.

## Working identity statement

**Public one-sentence version:**

Innercha explores inner motion through evolving rhythm, organic systems, and emotionally immersive sound.

**Longer public paragraph:**

Innercha explores the hidden motion that connects inner experience with the living systems around us. It is rooted in transformation, perception, emotional depth, and the evolving patterns found in nature, technology, and sound. Rather than representing a single genre or aesthetic, Innercha is a framework for creative exploration — one that embraces movement over permanence and curiosity over certainty.

**Feel statement (a compass, not a tagline):**

Innercha invites people into an evolving world rather than simply presenting them with music.

Note: an earlier draft referenced "psychedelic bass" directly. Removed deliberately — naming a genre in the public statement recreates the same confinement problem that motivated leaving SubRoot.

This is a draft, not final public copy.

## Private notes: why the change (not public copy)

This section holds the SubRoot-comparison reasoning so it has a home without living inside public statements above.

Jeff's own framing, kept close to how he wrote it: SubRoot is more literal and, in practice, more confining for artistic expression and branding. Innercha feels more expansive and better suited to age well over a long career rather than staying tied to one sound or one era.

This is useful context for collaborators and for Jeff himself. It should not appear in public bios, artist statements, or announcement copy — once the transition is complete, Innercha should be able to just exist rather than continuously explaining itself relative to SubRoot.

## What Innercha is not

- A name that locks the music to one bass subgenre
- Aggressive branding for its own sake
- Generic neon “EDM” imagery
- A fictional persona disconnected from Jeff
- An attempt to deny the SubRoot era
- A promise that every release sounds the same

## Visual direction

### Working mood

Premium, atmospheric, restrained, nature-informed, contemporary, and quietly psychedelic.

### Candidate palette

- charcoal and near-black
- fog blue
- muted forest or moss green
- sandstone
- dusk orange or restrained amber
- off-white

Keep the palette flexible until real artwork tests prove what works.

### Typography

Explore:

- clean geometric sans serif
- elegant grotesk or neo-grotesk
- restrained condensed display face for lineups
- simple title case or all caps: `Innercha` / `INNERCHA`

Avoid forced capitalization and novelty lettering that harms readability.

### Imagery

Explore:

- atmospheric landscapes
- topographic contours
- macro natural textures
- fog, moonlight, water, and mineral forms
- subtle motion blur or flow fields
- restrained geometric overlays

## Logo approach

Start with a strong wordmark before designing a symbol. The name already has useful rhythm and letter shape. A mark should emerge from repeated use rather than become a launch blocker.

## Voice

The written voice should be:

- thoughtful
- direct
- curious
- confident without hype
- personal without oversharing
- artist-led rather than marketing-led

## Verbal identity and pronunciation

- SubRoot has caused repeated "what?" reactions when said aloud in person, including at June Jam. Likely a mix of loud-venue acoustics and an unfamiliar word shape rather than one single clear cause.
- Innercha is one syllable longer than SubRoot, so it should get the same real-world test: say it aloud to people who haven't seen it written, and see how they spell or hear it back before finalizing wordmark direction.
- A rehearsed one-line verbal intro ("I'm Innercha — like inertia") is worth having regardless of the written brand; it gives listeners an anchor word instead of a cold new sound.
- "Nercha" is a candidate informal spoken nickname for loud or live settings. Not to be formalized as a handle, logo, or written brand element — let it develop organically if it does.
